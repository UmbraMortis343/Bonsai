topic: Leadership
tier: Leading a team
order: 2
source: Competency frameworks, behavioural interview questions and team-effectiveness research

graph: Direction
Direction | Making sure the team knows where it is going and why
  Team purpose | A plain statement of what the team exists to do and for whom
  Setting team goals | A few outcomes the whole team can name
    Outcomes over outputs | Measure the change you want, not the amount of activity
  Clarifying roles | Everyone knows what they own and who decides
    RACI | Who is responsible, accountable, consulted and informed for each piece of work
    ! Kick off a new project | Purpose, goals, roles, how you'll work, and the first milestone
  Explaining the why | People commit to reasons, not instructions

graph: Delegation
Delegation | Handing over work with the authority to do it
  Levels of delegation | From "do exactly this" to "decide and tell me after"
  Situational leadership | Match direction and support to the person's skill and confidence on that task
    Directing to delegating | Four styles: directing, coaching, supporting, delegating
  Delegating outcomes | Hand over the result wanted and the limits, not the method
    ! Delegate a task you'd rather keep | Outcome, limits, check-in points, and what support they want
  Micromanagement | Controlling the how, which removes ownership and learning
  Accountability | Following up on agreed commitments without taking the work back

graph: Developing people
Developing people | Helping each person get better at work that matters to them
  One-to-ones | A regular private meeting that belongs to the team member
  Coaching | Helping someone find their own answer through questions
    GROW model | Goal, reality, options, will: a simple order for a coaching conversation
    ! Coach instead of telling | Ask what they want, what's happening, what they could do, what they will do
  Motivation | Why people put in effort
    Intrinsic motivators | Autonomy, mastery and purpose sustain effort better than reward
    Hygiene factors | Pay and conditions prevent dissatisfaction but don't motivate by themselves
  Recognition | Noticing good work specifically and soon
  Underperformance | Name the gap early, find the cause, agree a plan, follow up
    ! Handle a missed deadline pattern | Facts first, ask for their account, agree what changes and when you'll check

graph: Team dynamics
Team dynamics | How a team behaves together
  Building trust | People rely on each other after seeing competence, honesty and care
    Vulnerability-based trust | Admitting weakness and asking for help without fear
  Five dysfunctions | Absence of trust, fear of conflict, lack of commitment, avoidance of accountability, inattention to results
  Productive conflict | Open debate about ideas, kept away from personalities
    Conflict modes | Competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding, accommodating
    ! Mediate between two members | Hear each alone, bring them together on the shared goal, agree specific behaviours
  Team norms | Agreed ways of working, set together and enforced by everyone
  Inclusion | Making sure every voice is heard and every person can contribute fully
  Groupthink | Agreement for the sake of harmony that hides doubts

graph: Running the work
Running the work | Keeping a team's effort organised and visible
  Effective meetings | A purpose, the right people, an agenda, and decisions written down
    Decision rights | Being clear whether you are informing, consulting or deciding together
  Planning | Break the goal into milestones, owners and dates
    Risks and contingencies | Name what could go wrong and what you'd do
    ! Plan an event with volunteers | Work back from the date, assign owners, build in slack, and confirm the week before
  Tracking progress | A shared view of what is done, doing and blocked
  Removing blockers | The leader's job is to clear the way
  Retrospectives | Regular reviews of what went well, what didn't and what to change

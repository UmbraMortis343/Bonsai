graph: Circular motion
Circular motion | Moving in a circle at steady speed while always accelerating
  Radian | The angle whose arc equals the radius
  Angular speed | Angle turned through per unit time, ω = 2π / T
    ! Using v = rω | Linear speed from radius and angular speed
  Centripetal acceleration | Directed to the centre: a = v² / r = rω²
  Centripetal force | The resultant force towards the centre that keeps a body in a circle
    ! Finding the centripetal force | F = mv² / r, provided by tension, friction or gravity

graph: Gravitational fields
Gravitational field | A region where a mass feels a force
  Newton's law of gravitation | F = GMm / r² between point masses
  Field strength of a point mass | g = GM / r²
    ! g at a planet's surface | Use the planet's mass and radius in g = GM / r²
  Gravitational potential | Work done per unit mass bringing a small mass from infinity: φ = −GM / r
    Gravitational potential energy of two masses | Eₚ = −GMm / r
  Circular orbits | Gravity provides the centripetal force
    ! Deriving T² ∝ r³ | Equate GMm / r² to mrω²
    Geostationary orbit | A 24-hour equatorial orbit that stays above one point

graph: Thermal physics and gases
Thermal equilibrium | No net energy flows between bodies at the same temperature
  Thermodynamic temperature | The kelvin scale, which does not depend on any substance
  Specific latent heat | Energy to change the state of 1 kg at constant temperature
    ! Using E = mL | Energy for melting or boiling
  Ideal gas | A gas that obeys pV ∝ T exactly
    Mole and Avogadro constant | 6.02 × 10²³ particles in one mole
    Equation of state | pV = nRT = NkT
    ! Solving a gas problem | Use pV = nRT with temperature in kelvin
  Kinetic theory of gases | Pressure explained by molecules colliding elastically with the walls
    Kinetic theory assumptions | Negligible volume, no forces between molecules, random motion, elastic collisions
    Root-mean-square speed | The square root of the mean of the squared speeds
    Mean kinetic energy of a molecule | 3/2 kT, proportional to temperature
    Boltzmann constant | The gas constant per molecule, k = R / Nₐ
  Internal energy | The sum of the random kinetic and potential energies of the molecules
  First law of thermodynamics | ΔU = q + W: energy supplied by heating plus work done on the system
    Work done by a gas | W = pΔV at constant pressure
    ! Applying the first law | Track signs of q, W and ΔU through a change

graph: Oscillations
Oscillations | Repeated motion about an equilibrium position
  Simple harmonic motion | Acceleration is proportional to displacement and directed to the centre
    Angular frequency | ω = 2πf
    Defining equation of SHM | a = −ω²x
    ! Displacement, velocity, acceleration | x = x₀ sin ωt and v = ±ω√(x₀² − x²)
  Energy in SHM | Swaps between kinetic and potential; the total is ½mω²x₀²
  Damping | Resistive forces remove energy, so the amplitude decays
    Critical damping | Returns to rest in the shortest time without oscillating
  Forced oscillations | Driven by a periodic force at the driver's frequency
    Resonance | Amplitude peaks when the driving frequency matches the natural frequency

graph: Electric fields
Electric fields | Regions where a charge feels a force
  Electric field strength | Force per unit positive charge
  Uniform electric field | Between parallel plates: E = ΔV / Δd
    ! Charged particle between plates | Constant force gives a parabolic path
  Coulomb's law | F = Qq / (4πε₀r²) between point charges
  Field of a point charge | E = Q / (4πε₀r²)
  Electric potential | Work done per unit positive charge bringing a small charge from infinity
    Potential gradient | Field strength equals minus the potential gradient
    Electric potential energy of two charges | Eₚ = Qq / (4πε₀r)
  Fields compared | Gravitational and electric fields share the inverse-square form; only electric can repel

graph: Capacitance
Capacitance | Charge stored per unit potential difference
  Capacitors in series and parallel | Parallel capacitances add; in series the reciprocals add
    ! Combined capacitance | Work out the total for a network
  Energy stored in a capacitor | ½QV = ½CV², the area under a potential–charge graph
  Capacitor discharge | Charge, current and voltage all fall exponentially through a resistor
    Time constant | τ = RC, the time to fall to 1/e of the starting value
    ! Using x = x₀ e^(−t/RC) | Find the charge or voltage left after a time

graph: Magnetic fields
Magnetic fields | Regions where moving charges and currents feel a force
  Magnetic flux density | Force per unit current per unit length on a wire at right angles to the field
    ! Using F = BIL sin θ | Force on a current-carrying conductor
  Force on a moving charge | F = BQv sin θ, at right angles to the velocity
    Circular path in a field | The magnetic force acts as the centripetal force
    Velocity selector | Crossed electric and magnetic fields pass only one speed
  Hall effect | A voltage across a conductor carrying current in a magnetic field
    Hall probe | Uses the Hall voltage to measure flux density
  Forces between parallel currents | Like currents attract, opposite currents repel
  Magnetic flux | Flux density times the area it passes through at right angles
    Flux linkage | Flux times the number of turns
  Faraday's law | Induced e.m.f. is proportional to the rate of change of flux linkage
  Lenz's law | The induced e.m.f. opposes the change that produces it
    ! E.m.f. in a moving conductor | Rate at which the conductor cuts flux

graph: Alternating currents
Alternating current | A current that varies sinusoidally and reverses direction
  Peak and r.m.s. values | The r.m.s. value is the peak divided by √2 for a sine wave
    ! Mean power in a resistor | Half the peak power for a sinusoidal current
  Rectification | Turning alternating current into direct current
    Half-wave rectification | One diode passes only one half of each cycle
    Full-wave rectification | A four-diode bridge passes both halves in the same direction
  Smoothing | A capacitor across the load fills in the gaps between peaks

graph: Quantum physics
Quantum physics | Energy and matter in discrete packets
  Photon | A quantum of electromagnetic energy, E = hf
    Photon momentum | p = E / c
    Electronvolt | The energy gained by an electron crossing one volt
  Photoelectric effect | Light ejects electrons from a metal only above a threshold frequency
    Work function | The least energy needed to free an electron from the surface
    ! Using hf = Φ + ½mv²max | Photon energy, work function and maximum kinetic energy
    Why intensity fails | Brighter light gives more electrons, not more energetic ones
  Wave–particle duality | Light and matter show both wave and particle behaviour
    Electron diffraction | Electrons make diffraction rings, so particles behave as waves
    de Broglie wavelength | λ = h / p
  Energy levels in atoms | Electrons occupy discrete energy states
    Line spectra | Each line is a photon emitted or absorbed between two levels
    ! Using hf = E₁ − E₂ | Wavelength of a spectral line from a level difference

graph: Nuclear physics
Nuclear energy | Energy bound up in the mass of nuclei
  Mass–energy equivalence | E = mc²
  Mass defect | A nucleus has less mass than its separate nucleons
  Binding energy | The energy needed to separate a nucleus into its nucleons
    Binding energy per nucleon | Peaks near iron, which is why fission and fusion both release energy
    ! Energy released in a reaction | Mass difference times c²
  Random and spontaneous decay | Unpredictable for one nucleus and unaffected by conditions
  Decay constant | The probability per unit time that a nucleus decays
    Activity | Decays per second: A = λN
    Exponential decay law | N = N₀ e^(−λt)
    ! Using λ = 0.693 / t½ | Link the decay constant and the half-life

graph: Medical physics
Medical physics | Physics used to look inside the body
  Ultrasound scanning | Pulses reflect at tissue boundaries and the echoes build an image
    Piezoelectric transducer | A crystal that turns voltage into vibration and back
    Acoustic impedance | Density times speed of sound; the mismatch sets how much reflects
    ! Reflected intensity fraction | (Z₁ − Z₂)² / (Z₁ + Z₂)²
  X-ray production | Fast electrons stopped by a metal target emit X-rays
    X-ray attenuation | Intensity falls exponentially with thickness: I = I₀ e^(−μx)
    CT scanning | Many X-ray views combined by computer into slices
  PET scanning | A tracer emits positrons; annihilation gamma pairs locate it
    Annihilation | A particle and its antiparticle convert to two gamma photons

graph: Astronomy and cosmology
Astronomy | Measuring stars and the Universe from their light
  Luminosity | Total power radiated by a star
  Standard candles | Objects of known luminosity used to find distance
    Inverse square law of flux | F = L / (4πd²)
    ! Distance from flux | Rearrange F = L / (4πd²) for a standard candle
  Wien's displacement law | Peak wavelength is inversely proportional to temperature
  Stefan–Boltzmann law | L = 4πσr²T⁴
    ! Radius of a star | From its luminosity and surface temperature
  Hubble's law | Recession speed is proportional to distance, v = H₀d
    Redshift and recession speed | Δλ / λ ≈ v / c
    Age of the Universe | Roughly 1 / H₀

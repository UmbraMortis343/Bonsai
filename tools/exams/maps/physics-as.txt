graph: Quantities and units
Physical quantities | A numerical size with a unit
  SI base units | kg, m, s, A, K and mol, from which other units are built
    Derived units | Products and quotients of base units, such as N = kg m s⁻²
    ! Checking homogeneity | Both sides of an equation must have the same base units
    Prefixes | Powers of ten from pico to tera
  Uncertainty | The range within which the true value is expected to lie
    Systematic and random errors | A consistent offset, or scatter about the true value
    Accuracy and precision | Closeness to the true value, and closeness of repeats to each other
    ! Combining uncertainties | Add absolute uncertainties for sums, percentage ones for products
  Resolving vectors | Splitting a vector into two perpendicular components
    ! Finding components | F cos θ along and F sin θ across

graph: Kinematics
Kinematics | Describing motion without asking what causes it
  Displacement | Distance in a stated direction from a starting point
  Motion graphs | Gradient of displacement–time is velocity; area under velocity–time is displacement
  Equations of uniform acceleration | The suvat equations for constant acceleration in a straight line
    ! Solving with suvat | Pick the equation that has the three knowns and the one unknown
    ! Measuring g by free fall | Time a fall over a known height and use s = ½gt²
  Projectile motion | Constant horizontal velocity with uniform vertical acceleration
    Independence of components | Horizontal and vertical motions do not affect each other
    ! Range of a projectile | Find the time of flight vertically, then the distance horizontally

graph: Dynamics
Dynamics | Why things move as they do
  Newton's first law | A body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless a resultant force acts
    Inertia | Mass resists change in motion
  Newton's second law | Resultant force equals the rate of change of momentum
    Linear momentum | The product of mass and velocity, a vector
    ! Force from a change in momentum | F = Δp / Δt
  Newton's third law | Forces come in equal and opposite pairs acting on different bodies
  Motion with resistance | Drag grows with speed until it balances the driving force
  Elastic and inelastic collisions | Kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic ones
    Relative speed of approach | Equals the relative speed of separation in an elastic collision
    ! Two-body collision in one dimension | Conserve momentum, then check kinetic energy

graph: Forces in equilibrium
Equilibrium | No resultant force and no resultant torque
  Couple | Two equal, opposite, parallel forces that only turn
    Torque of a couple | One force times the perpendicular distance between them
  Triangle of forces | Three forces in equilibrium form a closed triangle
    ! Resolving for equilibrium | Components in each direction sum to zero
  Upthrust | The upward force on a body in a fluid, from the pressure difference across it
    Archimedes' principle | Upthrust equals the weight of fluid displaced
    Hydrostatic pressure | The derivation of Δp = ρgΔh from the weight of a column

graph: Work and power
Work and energy transfer | Work done is energy moved by a force
  Work by a force at an angle | W = Fs cos θ
  Deriving kinetic energy | ½mv² follows from the equations of motion and W = Fs
  Deriving potential energy | mgh from the work done against weight in a uniform field
  Power and velocity | P = Fv for a force moving at speed v
    ! Power of a moving vehicle | Driving force times velocity at steady speed
  Efficiency of a system | Useful output over total input, with losses usually as heat

graph: Deformation of solids
Deformation | Change of shape under tensile or compressive forces
  Hooke's law | Force is proportional to extension up to the limit of proportionality
  Stress | Force per unit cross-sectional area
  Strain | Extension per unit original length
  Young modulus | Stress over strain: the stiffness of a material
    ! Measuring the Young modulus | Load a long thin wire, measure extension and diameter
  Elastic and plastic deformation | Elastic recovers fully, plastic leaves a permanent change
    Elastic limit | The point beyond which deformation is permanent
  Elastic potential energy | The area under a force–extension graph
    ! Using E = ½Fx | Energy stored in a spring that obeys Hooke's law

graph: Progressive waves
Progressive waves | Waves that carry energy from place to place
  Phase difference | How far out of step two points or two waves are, as an angle
  Intensity | Power per unit area, proportional to amplitude squared
    ! Intensity and amplitude | Doubling the amplitude quadruples the intensity
  Cathode-ray oscilloscope | Shows a wave as a trace, to read period and amplitude
  Doppler effect | Observed frequency changes when the source moves relative to the observer
    ! Doppler shift for sound | f₀ = f v / (v ± vₛ)
  Polarisation | Vibration confined to one plane; only transverse waves show it
    Malus's law | Transmitted intensity is I₀ cos²θ

graph: Superposition
Superposition | Where waves meet, their displacements add
  Stationary waves | Formed by two identical waves travelling in opposite directions
    Nodes and antinodes | Points of zero and of maximum amplitude
    ! Harmonics on a string | A whole number of half-wavelengths fits the length
  Interference | The pattern made by superposing coherent waves
    Coherence | A constant phase difference between sources
    Path difference | Whole wavelengths give maxima, half wavelengths give minima
  Double-slit experiment | Two slits give evenly spaced bright and dark fringes
    ! Using λ = ax / D | Wavelength from slit separation, fringe spacing and screen distance
  Diffraction grating | Many slits give sharp, bright maxima
    ! Using d sin θ = nλ | Angles of the maxima from a grating

graph: Current electricity
Charge carriers | The moving charges that make up a current
  Quantised charge | Charge comes in multiples of e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
  Drift velocity | The slow average speed of carriers along a conductor
    ! Using I = Anvq | Current from area, number density, drift speed and charge
  Volt | One joule per coulomb
  Ohm's law | Current is proportional to potential difference at constant temperature
  Resistivity | A material property: R = ρL / A
    ! Resistance from resistivity | Find the resistance of a wire from its dimensions
  Filament lamp behaviour | Resistance rises as the filament heats up
  Power dissipation | P = I²R = V² / R

graph: D.C. circuits
D.C. circuits | Steady currents round networks of components
  Internal resistance | Resistance inside a source that lowers its terminal voltage
    Terminal potential difference | E.m.f. minus the lost volts, V = E − Ir
    ! Finding internal resistance | Plot V against I: gradient −r, intercept E
  Kirchhoff's first law | Currents into a junction equal currents out: charge is conserved
  Kirchhoff's second law | E.m.f.s round a loop equal the potential drops: energy is conserved
    ! Analysing a two-loop circuit | Write a junction equation and two loop equations
  Deriving resistor combinations | Series and parallel formulas follow from Kirchhoff's laws
  Potentiometer | A potential divider used to compare potential differences by a null method
    Galvanometer null | No current flows when the two potential differences match

graph: Particle physics
Particle physics | What matter is made of at the smallest scale
  Alpha-scattering experiment | A few alpha particles bounced back, so the atom has a tiny dense nucleus
  Nuclear equations | Nucleon number and charge are conserved in every reaction
  Antiparticles | Same mass, opposite charge; the positron is the electron's
  Beta decay | A neutron becomes a proton (β⁻) or a proton a neutron (β⁺), with a neutrino
    Neutrinos | Almost massless neutral particles that explain beta decay's spread of energies
  Fundamental particles | Quarks and leptons have no known structure
    Quarks | Six flavours: up, down, strange, charm, top, bottom, with charges of ⅔e or −⅓e
    Leptons | Electrons, neutrinos and their heavier relatives
    Hadrons | Particles made of quarks: baryons have three, mesons a quark and antiquark
    ! Quark make-up of nucleons | Proton uud, neutron udd; check the charges add up
  Unified atomic mass unit | One twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom
